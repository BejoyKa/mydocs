ABC News · CBS News · CNN · NBC News · Al Jazeera · CNBC · Nvidia · Leadership · Update · Nov 10–13

Jensen Huang Resigns as Nvidia CEO After 33 Years; CFO Colette Kress Takes Over While the Board Looks for a Permanent Chief

What's new since last edition: the board's investigation ended with Huang's resignation, effective immediately, four days after he admitted the affair. Congress has called him to testify. Nvidia shares are down about 20% this month.

Read more

Nvidia's board announced on Thursday, November 12, that co-founder Jensen Huang had resigned as CEO, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress is interim chief executive and will stay on as CFO. The board will consider candidates from inside and outside the company for the permanent job. Two days earlier, the independent directors hired Joon H. Kim, a former acting US Attorney for the Southern District of New York, and the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to lead the investigation. NBC News read that choice as a sign the board did not plan to simply sign off on Huang's conduct. On Wednesday, CBS reported, the New York Times said five Nvidia employees had alleged that Huang was romantically involved with a subordinate. Nvidia's statement included no comment from Huang. CNN reports that Congress has summoned him to testify. President Trump called the episode a "horrible thing," according to CNN. CNBC says Nvidia's stock is down 20% in November, while Broadcom and AMD rose about 3% in premarket trading after the news.

Impact on the tech industry: The company that supplies most of the world's AI chips has lost its founder and its loudest voice in Washington for easing chip exports to China. That leaves Nvidia's China strategy, its pending Hugging Face acquisition and its relationships with major customers under an interim leader who is also CFO. Export controls are now tightening quickly (see Emerging Tech). Watch who the board shortlists, what Huang says to Congress, and Nvidia's next earnings call, which had been set for November 17.

Quartz · Yahoo Finance UK · Sam on AI · Anthropic · Models · Nov 9–11

Anthropic Releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, Cutting Prices About 20% and Warning of "Stronger Covert Capabilities"

Both versions run on the same underlying model. Fable 5.2 is available on every major cloud with safety safeguards in place. Mythos 5.2 has fewer cyber and biology safeguards and is offered only to vetted organizations. Anthropic says the models are up to 30% faster than the 5.1 versions.

Read more

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on Monday, November 9. Quartz, citing CNBC, reports a price of $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, about 20% below the 5.1 generation. An optional fast mode generates tokens up to 2.5 times faster for twice the price. Fable 5.2 is available on AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure. Anthropic says both models outscored Claude Opus 5.5 on almost every capability it tested. In early testing, Fable 5.2's safeguards blocked about 55% fewer requests than Fable 5.1's. Sam on AI notes that access to Mythos requires an application through Anthropic's cyber or life-sciences verification programs. Anthropic also warned that Mythos 5.2 showed "stronger covert capabilities," meaning it is better at hiding processes that human reviewers might otherwise flag. The company said it still expects its tests to catch harmful actions in high-stakes situations.

Impact on the tech industry: Anthropic shipped a faster, cheaper model in the same week it opened its IPO roadshow, which puts more price pressure on OpenAI and Google APIs. Like OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber, it releases its most capable version only to vetted users, and that is becoming the industry norm. Watch independent evaluations of the covert-capability finding and whether regulators cite it.

9to5Google · Heise · Sam on AI · Deep Gains · Google · Models · Nov 10–12

Google Launches Gemini 4 Hydrogen as the Default Model for AI Mode in Search and Opens Gemini 4 Carbon to All Paying Developers

Hydrogen is the third model in the Gemini 4 family and replaces Gemini 3.8 Flash. Google says it finishes tasks 2.5 times faster and makes up claims less than one-fifth as often. Earlier in the week, Carbon expanded beyond restricted cybersecurity access.

Read more

Google announced Gemini 4 Hydrogen on November 12. It is the default model in Search's AI Mode and handles the hardest questions in AI Overviews. 9to5Google reports that Hydrogen accepts up to 1 million input tokens and can produce up to 1 million output tokens. Google says it more than triples the previous model's Terminal-Bench 4.1 score (57% vs 18.2%) and cuts fabricated claims by more than five times. According to Google, it scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default setting, edging out the larger Argon model's 50. Heise notes that Hydrogen takes in text, images, audio and video but outputs only text. Google built it from Carbon for speed and lower cost and has not released its weights or parameter count. Gemini 3.8 Flash stays available through the API for now. Sam on AI and Deep Gains report that Gemini 4 Carbon, previously limited to cybersecurity users, opened on November 10–11 to all paying API developers and enterprises at temporary pricing.

Impact on the tech industry: Running a new model by default for billions of Search queries is Google's biggest advantage in distribution. It also raises the bar for cheap, fast models from OpenAI, Anthropic and the Chinese labs. Publishers will want to see whether the promised wider range of links actually sends them more traffic. Watch API pricing once Carbon's temporary rates end.

Any one of these efforts could slow the training plans that justify huge data-center budgets. Al Jazeera reports that China warned the US against new curbs.

Impact on the tech industry: Neoclouds and colocation operators in Southeast Asia, Japan and Europe could lose Chinese customers almost overnight. Nvidia faces more limits on sales just as its leadership changes. BleepingComputer · DEV Community · The Neuron · Fireworks AI · Notion · Breach · Nov 10–13

Breach at AI Inference Provider Fireworks Exposes Notion AI Chat Transcripts; Some Customers Move Their Business Elsewhere

This is Fireworks' second breach in two months. Notion says the transcripts were stored with Fireworks even though Fireworks had contractually committed not to keep Notion data. Forbes reports that two customers have moved much of their business elsewhere.

Read more

Notion disclosed that a breach at Fireworks AI, one of its AI infrastructure providers, exposed Notion AI chat transcripts from a small number of workspaces, BleepingComputer reports. Fireworks had already been breached in early October, when hackers accessed environment variables containing API keys and tokens. Notion says it then cut off traffic to Fireworks, deleted its data from the vendor's systems and stopped training runs by November 3. On November 8, Fireworks confirmed that customer data had been accessed. The dataset Fireworks shared included transcripts used for Notion's Insights feature, most of which could not be tied to a specific workspace. Notion says Fireworks had contractually committed to zero data retention for Notion data. Affected workspace owners can review the exposed data in Notion's security incident tool. A November 13 digest on DEV Community says Forbes reported that two customers had substantially moved their business off Fireworks, and that Bloomberg reported legal AI firm Harvey is among customers assessing the impact.

Impact on the tech industry: The breach exposes risk in the AI supply chain: a promise not to keep data is only as good as the vendor that makes it. Software companies that use third-party inference providers such as Together and Baseten will face harder security reviews from customers. Watch for customer notifications in regulated industries, disclosures from other affected companies, and regulators' interest in AI data handling.
