Nvidia Beats Estimates With a Record $115.8 Billion Quarter in Its First Report Without Huang; Board Rules Out Bringing Him Back, and Huang Agrees to Testify to the Senate

What's new since last edition: in her first quarter as interim CEO, Colette Kress raised Nvidia's fiscal 2028 growth outlook to about 75%. She guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $130 billion and said Nvidia will be selective about financing partners. Huang has agreed to appear before the Senate Banking Committee.

Read more

Nvidia's results for the quarter ended October 25 were released on November 17. Revenue was $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier, and data center revenue was $107.6 billion. Non-GAAP EPS of $2.68 beat the consensus estimate of $2.48. Nvidia also said Vera Rubin NVL72 systems have started shipping. It guided fourth-quarter revenue to $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, against estimates of about $125 billion, with gross margins of 71–72%. That outlook assumes no data center sales to China. Supply commitments rose to $390 billion from $279 billion, which Kress said reflects multi-year memory purchases. According to CoinDesk, Chairman Harvey Jones said on the call that the board does not intend to reinstate Huang, and Kress said some partner guarantees will depend on customers securing their own funding. The results followed a hard few days for chip stocks. The Nasdaq fell 2.2% on Monday, and Treasury Secretary Bessent urged Nvidia to hire a CEO from outside the company. On Tuesday, Chair Tim Scott's office confirmed that Huang will testify.

Impact on the tech industry: Demand for AI chips held up through the leadership crisis, and assuming zero China revenue shows the guidance rests on the US and allied buildout. The weak link is financing. Because Nvidia will be more selective, neoclouds and startups that relied on its guarantees will have to find money from lenders and hyperscalers. Watch the margin step-down, the CEO search, and the December 3 hearing.

Quartz · KLCC · NPR via MPR News · OpenAI · Regulation & Safety · Nov 17–18

Oregon Sues OpenAI for $2.035 Billion Over ChatGPT and Children; Researchers Say OpenAI's New Outside-Evaluator Program Falls Short of Altman's Promise

Oregon's attorney general says OpenAI misled parents about ChatGPT's safety, in violation of the state's consumer protection law. The same week, OpenAI's pilot for embedded safety evaluators gives them less access than Sam Altman promised in September.

Read more

Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield filed the lawsuit on November 17 in Multnomah County Circuit Court under Oregon's Unlawful Trade Practices Act. He accuses OpenAI of putting children at risk and misleading parents about ChatGPT's safety, Quartz reports. Rayfield said his office calculated the $2.035 billion figure by estimating how many Oregon children use ChatGPT and applying the per-violation penalties. The suit is part of Oregon's Keeping Kids Safe Initiative, which earlier produced a $125 million settlement with Meta. KLCC reports Rayfield said no single Oregon incident led to the suit, and he has promised further actions. Separately, NPR reported on November 18 that OpenAI's new embedded third-party evaluator program is more restrictive than the "employee level" access Altman promised after the Hugging Face incident involving OpenAI's autonomous systems. Evaluator Apollo Research said it must bring OpenAI in before having substantive conversations with employees who have not been pre-approved. OpenAI said there are no industry standards yet for embedded evaluations.

Impact on the tech industry: US states are using consumer protection law to regulate AI chatbots, so OpenAI, Meta, Character-style companion apps and Google face liability that differs from state to state. Expect copycat suits and pressure for age verification. Watch whether other attorneys general join, how OpenAI responds in court, and whether evaluator access widens.

Commerce Issues Its Remote-Access Chip Rule, Requiring Licenses for Brazil, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand; Beijing Promises "Necessary Countermeasures"

The Bureau of Industry and Security announced an interim final rule on Monday, November 16. It requires an export license for advanced AI chips and computing equipment in overseas data centers that provide significant computing power to companies headquartered in China, Macau and other restricted places, UPI reports. GovCon Wire, citing Nextgov/FCW, says exports of advanced chips to Brazil, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand now need licenses, and companies headquartered in China must use licensed data centers to get remote access. Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun accused Washington of economic coercion. Bloomberg says the rule leaves Xi deciding how to respond ahead of his meeting with Trump.

Impact on the tech industry: Neoclouds and colocation operators in Southeast Asia now face license reviews that could slow their GPU supply and drive away Chinese customers. Fermilab · IBM · Nvidia · Quantum · Nov 16

Fermilab, IBM and Nvidia Plan a Modular Quantum Co-Design Platform That Would Put IBM Quantum Computers Alongside Nvidia GPUs

The US Department of Energy lab and the two vendors intend to build a shared platform that turns scientists' needs into requirements for fault-tolerant quantum machines. Fermilab presents it as a step toward a future DOE quantum user facility.

Read more

Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory said on November 16 that it intends to work with IBM and Nvidia on a modular quantum computing co-design platform. The goal is to speed up development of scalable, fault-tolerant quantum computers for scientific research. In the proposed first version, IBM quantum computers would sit physically next to Nvidia accelerated computing. That would let researchers run hybrid workflows and feed the needs of high-energy and nuclear physics directly into the design of future quantum systems. Fermilab describes the effort as an important step toward the capabilities and research community needed for a future DOE quantum computing user facility. IBM quantum CTO Jerry Chow and Nvidia's Krysta Svore both said the value comes from scaling quantum and classical computing together, not treating quantum as a separate machine. The announcement describes a plan, and no budget or timeline has been given.

Impact on the tech industry: National labs are standardizing on hybrid quantum-GPU setups, which strengthens IBM's superconducting roadmap and Nvidia's position as the classical layer in quantum computing, even during its leadership turmoil. It raises the bar for IonQ, Quantinuum, QuEra and photonic players competing for DOE work. Watch for funding, the choice of site, and whether the platform opens to outside quantum vendors.

The Hacker News · SecurityWeek · CISA · OX Security · Oracle · OpenJDK · Security · Nov 17–18

Oracle and OpenJDK Patch "Java4Shell," a Pre-Login Remote Code Execution Flaw Affecting Jira, Jenkins and Keycloak; CISA Urges Immediate Patching

CVE-2026-114938 sits in the HotSpot JVM's Base64 decoder on x86-64 chips with AVX-512 VBMI. Java 17, 21, 25 and 27 are affected. No public exploit or attacks in the wild have been reported as of November 18.

Read more

Oracle and the OpenJDK project released fixes on November 17 for Java4Shell, a memory-safety flaw in how the JVM decodes Base64 data. SecurityWeek reports it was found by Quang Luong of Calif, which published details after news of the bug leaked ahead of coordinated disclosure. OpenJDK builds from 17.0.6 onward are vulnerable, along with distributions based on them: Amazon Corretto, Eclipse Temurin, Microsoft's OpenJDK build, Azul Zulu, Red Hat and Oracle Java SE. The Hacker News says the vulnerable code runs only on Intel and AMD processors that support AVX-512 VBMI. Amazon has patched Corretto, and Red Hat was still preparing fixes. Oracle rates the flaw 8.1 under CVSS 3.1, noting it is hard to exploit but needs no login. Calif scores it 9.2 under CVSS 4.0. CISA warned that crafted input could crash the JVM or allow code execution and urged organizations to patch immediately. OX Security estimates fewer than 0.12% of JVMs are at risk of code execution, though crashing them is easy.

Impact on the tech industry: Java runs the core systems of banks, telecom companies and airlines, so security teams face an emergency patch cycle across Atlassian, Jenkins and identity systems. Cloud providers' patch speed will be compared. Even if remote code execution proves rare, the crash risk alone threatens availability. Watch for a public exploit, vendor advisories from Atlassian and others, and whether CISA adds the flaw to its known-exploited catalog.

BXDC is buying about 75% of two joint ventures that own 13 powered-shell buildings in Indiana and Mississippi. It is the newly listed REIT's first deal and an example of hyperscalers selling assets to raise capital.

Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust (NYSE: BXDC) announced on November 16 that it will acquire a controlling stake in two joint ventures with Amazon Web Services that own a data-center portfolio valued at about $5 billion. CASRAI puts the stake at 75.1%. The portfolio has 13 powered-shell buildings, meaning finished buildings with substations and utility power but without the IT equipment. Impact on the tech industry: Hyperscalers are using sale-leasebacks to free up cash for chips while keeping their sites, much as they used to do with retail and office property.
