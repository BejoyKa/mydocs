Nvidia Has Its Worst Week on Record as the Senate Sets December Hearings for Jensen Huang and Howard Lutnick; Board Reportedly Approaches Arm's Rene Haas

What's new since last edition: Nvidia shares fell 13% in the week Huang resigned, the sixth straight weekly decline. Senate Banking wants Huang to testify on December 3 and Commerce Secretary Lutnick on December 10. The search for a successor has begun, and some large shareholders want to leave the door open for Huang to return.

Read more

CNBC reports that Nvidia shares fell 13% in the week to Friday, November 13, the biggest drop since April 2025. It was the stock's sixth weekly decline in a row, the longest streak since 2012. Since early October the company has lost about 30% of its value, roughly $1.7 trillion, and it is now worth less than Apple. ThePrint says the loss for the week alone was about $573 billion, a record. Other estimates are higher. Senate Banking Chairman Tim Scott has asked Huang to appear on December 3 and said the committee could issue subpoenas if he refuses. CASRAI reports that Lutnick agreed to testify on December 10, and that Senators Warren and Coons had ordered officials to preserve Nvidia records after reports of a foreign intelligence operation that targeted Huang. On succession, digests citing press reports say the board approached Arm CEO Rene Haas (Reported). AI Frontier Daily, citing CNBC, says two large outside shareholders asked the board to keep Huang's return possible, while saying interim CEO Colette Kress can lead the company in the meantime. Nvidia says customer commitments and its product roadmap will continue without interruption.

Impact on the tech industry: The hearings make Nvidia's leadership crisis part of the debate over export controls. That comes as Commerce prepares its rule on remote chip access (see last edition), which still has not been published. Neoclouds that depend on Nvidia, such as CoreWeave, are already being sold off (see Emerging Tech). Watch whether the board names a permanent CEO, whether Nvidia keeps its earnings date, which had been set for November 17, and what Huang and Lutnick say in December.

TechCrunch · AI Frontier Daily · Lapaas Voice · OpenAI · Commerce · Nov 13–14

OpenAI Launches ChatGPT Wallet So Its "Dots" Agents Can Buy Things for Users, Starting With Some US Pro Subscribers

Agents can recommend products and then complete the checkout once the user approves it. The feature works with Visa, Mastercard and American Express cards. OpenAI also published a playbook to help merchants support shoppers who use agents.

Read more

On Friday, November 13, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet. It lets the company's agents, called dots, help users choose what to buy and then complete the purchase. TechCrunch reports that the first users are a limited number of US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and over who have a US-issued Visa, Mastercard or American Express card. Before checkout, users review the items, the total and the card that will be charged. The merchant receives the shipping address and contact details it needs to fulfill the order. AI Frontier Daily says the agents never see the actual card number and that users approve purchases with a fingerprint or face scan. OpenAI plans to add delegated purchasing later, where a user sets a spending limit and category once instead of approving each purchase. OpenAI also published a Commerce Partner Playbook to help retailers recognize legitimate agent traffic instead of blocking it as bot traffic. The feature builds on the Business Hub and Business Agents covered last edition.

Impact on the tech industry: With Wallet, OpenAI now covers the full shopping process, from finding products to paying for them. That puts pressure on Google Shopping, Amazon and checkout providers such as Shop Pay and PayPal. Card networks benefit because they supply the payment rails. Lapaas Voice reports that Amazon plans a global product database to keep control of product discovery as AI shopping agents spread (Reported). Watch the wider rollout, fraud and chargeback rates, and whether retailers that block bots, like United, change their position.

Fortune · AI Weekly · DEV Community · AI Frontier Daily · OpenAI · Robotics · Nov 13–15

OpenAI Disbands Its Stand-Alone Robotics Team, Saying Its General-Purpose Models Can Now Control Robots Directly

Robotics researchers now report to Chief Research Officer Mark Chen. The applied robotics and hardware staff move to Greg Brockman's Applied organization. OpenAI says the change is meant to speed up its robotics work, not end it, and it still plans to show a humanoid robot publicly.

Read more

OpenAI confirmed to Fortune on November 13 that it has broken up its stand-alone robotics team. Robotics research has moved into the main research group under Mark Chen. The applied robotics and hardware teams have moved into the larger Applied organization, which reports to President Greg Brockman. OpenAI said it had expected earlier this year to build robotics on top of video models like Sora. Since then, it said, general-purpose frontier models such as GPT-6 Astra have proved they can be applied to robots directly. AI Weekly reports that OpenAI justified the change with a benchmark: GPT-6 Astra scored 74.8% on simulated robotics tasks, compared with 51% for specialized models. This is the second time OpenAI has dissolved a robotics team. It closed the first one in 2020, citing a lack of robot data, rebuilt a team in 2024, and ended its partnership with Figure in February 2025. AI Frontier Daily, citing Fortune, says OpenAI still plans to show a humanoid robot publicly, a goal Sam Altman has targeted for 2027.

Impact on the tech industry: If one general model can run robots, companies that build specialized robot "brains" will find it harder to stand out, and the value shifts toward hardware, data and deployment. Figure, Physical Intelligence, Skild and Nvidia's robotics stack all compete for that layer. Investors in humanoid startups such as Agile Robots, which just raised $500 million (see Funding), will want to see real-world results. Watch for robotics features in OpenAI's general models and independent tests of the Astra benchmark claims.

NewSpace Economy · Space.com · KeepTrack · SpaceX · Starship · Update · Nov 13–15

Starship Flight 15: SpaceX Catches the Ship Back From Orbit With the Tower Arms for the First Time but Loses the Booster

What's new since last edition: the planned double catch half worked. Ship 42 reached orbit, deployed Starlink satellites and was caught by the tower after about 22.5 hours. The Super Heavy booster called off its catch and was lost in the Gulf of Mexico.

Read more

Starship lifted off from Starbase, Texas, at 22:47 UTC on November 13 carrying Starlink Group 31-2, KeepTrack reports. The ship reached orbit and deployed its payload. NewSpace Economy reports that Ship 42 then completed a flight of about 22.5 hours and returned to Starbase on November 14. There it was caught by the tower's mechanical arms, the first tower catch of a Starship returning from orbit. The Super Heavy booster called off its catch and diverted offshore. Its flight termination system activated, and it was lost in the Gulf of Mexico. Space.com reports that SpaceX is still investigating why. During the webcast, SpaceX said the booster's guidance, navigation and control system had actually performed well, so the cause has not been confirmed. KeepTrack notes that every missed booster catch means another booster must be built before the next flight.

Impact on the tech industry: Catching a ship that has returned from orbit is the main step toward a fully reusable heavy launcher. That would lower the cost of putting satellites in orbit, both for Starlink and for SpaceX's planned Starmind orbital-compute constellation. Rivals such as Blue Origin, Rocket Lab and China's state launchers face a tougher cost comparison. Watch the cause of the booster failure, how quickly Ship 42 can fly again, and how much time passes before Flight 16.

Cybersecurity News · ChainCatcher via KuCoin · Clear Intelligence · The Neuron · Fireworks AI · Cursor · Uber · Breach · Update · Nov 13–15

Fireworks Breach Spreads: Cursor Traces Its Poisoned Claude Code Extension to a Leaked Fireworks Key, and Uber Confirms Driver Data Was Exposed

What's new since last edition: two more companies have linked incidents to the breach at AI inference provider Fireworks. Cursor says its tampered extension was available for 11 hours and was downloaded by about 5,000 developers. Uber says personal data of thousands of US drivers held by Fireworks was accessed.

Read more

Cursor published a full post-mortem on November 14 of the incident it first disclosed on October 29. A malicious, unauthorized copy of Anthropic's Claude Code extension had appeared in Cursor's VS Code extension marketplace. Cybersecurity News reports that Cursor traced the breach to an access key exposed by Fireworks AI. ChainCatcher, via KuCoin, explains that the key was not meant for the marketplace. The attackers used it to get into Cursor's internal systems, collect more credentials, including keys that controlled the marketplace, and publish the tampered extension to its cache. The extension stayed up for about 11 hours, and roughly 5,000 developers downloaded it. Cursor tells affected users to reinstall their operating systems and rotate all credentials, because uninstalling the extension does not remove the persistence scripts it planted. Uber has separately confirmed that hackers accessed personal information of thousands of US drivers held by Fireworks. Notion's disclosure, covered last edition, affected fewer than 1% of its workspaces, according to The Neuron.

Impact on the tech industry: One inference vendor's leaked secrets have now led to a developer-tool supply-chain attack and a consumer-data breach. The case for treating AI providers as critical third parties is getting stronger. Expect more scrutiny of Together, Baseten and other inference hosts. Code-signing and extension-marketplace security, including Microsoft's Visual Studio Marketplace and Open VSX, will also get more attention. Watch for other customers to disclose incidents, lawsuits against Fireworks, and action from regulators.

Samsung Electronics is in talks to acquire Modine Manufacturing, a cooling-systems maker based in Racine, Wisconsin, according to Bloomberg reporting cited by DCD and Yahoo Finance.
