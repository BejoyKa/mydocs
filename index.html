TechCrunch · CNBC · Anthropic · IPO · Oct 26

Anthropic Files Publicly for Nasdaq IPO as "ANT": $16.3 Billion in First-Half Revenue, an $80.7 Billion Loss, and a Tech Rally

The S-1 shows first-half revenue up about 1,300% from $1.2 billion a year ago, more than 14,000 enterprise customers, and a governance structure that keeps a trust and the co-founders in control. Chip stocks rallied on the filing.

Read more

After weeks of leaked drafts, Anthropic filed its S-1 publicly on October 26. It plans to list Class A shares on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under "ANT." First-half 2026 revenue was $16.3 billion, up from $1.2 billion a year earlier. The net loss was $80.7 billion, but $72.4 billion of that is a non-cash charge from changes in the fair value of convertible notes, derivatives and SAFEs. The operating loss narrowed to $693 million from $2.1 billion, and the second quarter showed $974 million in operating income. Anthropic reports more than 14,000 enterprise customers, about 250 of which each bring in $10 million or more a year. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead 24 underwriters. Both the company and existing holders will sell shares, and the share count and price have not been set. Anthropic was last valued at $965 billion in May. NPR cites reports of a $1.5–2 trillion IPO valuation. Risk factors include catastrophic AI risk and the company's dispute with the Trump administration and the Defense Department. On Monday the S&P 500 rose 0.70% and the Nasdaq 0.82%.

Impact on the tech industry: This is the first full public look at a frontier lab's finances, and it gives investors a benchmark for OpenAI, xAI and the rest of the AI stack. Operating profitability at this scale undercuts the "no business model" bear case. The size of the paper loss shows how complicated these capital structures are. Watch for price-range setting, how much of the book goes to institutions, and whether Anthropic's coding-price fight with OpenAI shows up in amended filings.

OpenAI · Anthropic · Research · Oct 26–27

OpenAI and Anthropic Each Say Their AI Disproved the Exponential Time Hypothesis, Working Independently

Both labs say an internal model found a subexponential-time algorithm for 3-SAT on October 16 and published Lean proofs. That would overturn a nearly 30-year-old assumption behind hundreds of complexity results. Outside verification is still under way.

Read more

On October 26, OpenAI announced three results it says its internal models produced. The first is a proof of the local smoothing conjecture in every dimension, which OpenAI says also settles the Kakeya and Stein restriction conjectures. The second refutes the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH). The third is an existence proof in quantum chromodynamics that covers pure Yang–Mills theory. OpenAI says the ETH disproof was written on October 16 and formalized in Lean the next day. Both versions are on its public GitHub. According to OfficeChai, OpenAI has agreed to release the raw data and fund a conference at the request of a verification group. Hours later, Anthropic published "A 2^o(n) Time Algorithm for 3-SAT," credited to Claude. It says the core algorithm was also found on October 16, using 14 million output tokens and no human input, and it added a Lean 4 formalization on October 27. The paper says the result also refutes the stronger SETH, but not the Orthogonal Vectors hypothesis. It counts more than 800 papers that rely on ETH. Both labs say practical speedups will take substantial further work.

Impact on the tech industry: If the results hold up, frontier models will have moved from helping with research to producing major theoretical results, and that changes the case for funding AI-for-science. Cryptography, SAT-solver and optimization vendors will need to look again at their hardness assumptions. Watch for independent peer review, any flaws found in the Lean proofs, and whether Google DeepMind makes a similar claim.

CISA · PHP2Shell · Security · Update · Oct 26–27

PHP2Shell Update: CISA Adds Both Flaws to Its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog After Remote Code Execution Is Confirmed

What's new: the watch item from last edition has been resolved. CISA listed CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 on Monday after a Perplexity researcher showed working remote code execution against honeypots.

Read more

The Hacker News reports that on October 26, CISA added both PHP2Shell flaws to its KEV catalog. Each is rated CVSS 9.2 and allows unauthenticated remote code execution. CVE-2026-109618 is an out-of-memory corruption bug, and CVE-2026-109619 is a stale internal request-state bug. The listing followed honeypot evidence from Adel Karimi, detection and response lead at Perplexity, who confirmed successful exploitation on October 24. His evidence included a large memory dump and a function address that was then used in the payload. Karimi cautioned that he showed the chain only on a deliberately favorable single-worker mod_php setup, and that it does not measure how reliable the exploit is on other configurations. Fixed builds are PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35. Brandefense says 59% of the sites checked with its tool still showed a pre-fix PHP version. Tenable, SOCRadar and Greenbone have published remediation guidance.

Impact on the tech industry: A KEV listing triggers mandatory patch deadlines for US federal agencies and usually speeds up enterprise patching too. It is also a sign that insurers and auditors will start asking questions. Hosting companies, WordPress agencies and managed security providers carry the most exposure. Watch for confirmed breach disclosures and whether attackers make the exploit work on multi-worker setups.

The offer covers staff at vice president level and below on selected teams in the US, Canada, India and the UK, including Xbox, Windows, Copilot and Finance. Data-center, sales and marketing staff are excluded.

Oracle · AI World · Oct 26–27

Oracle Opens AI World With a "Deep Trust" Architecture That Enforces Rules on AI Agents at the Database Layer

On day one in Las Vegas, Oracle pitched security, privacy and business rules built into the data stack instead of relying on each agent to follow them. It also expanded its Autonomous AI Lakehouse.

Read more

Oracle opened AI World in Las Vegas on October 26, Channel Insider reports. The headline announcement was the Oracle Deep Trust AI Architecture, a framework that puts security, privacy and business rules into the technology stack itself instead of leaving them to individual agents or apps. It covers four layers: software platforms, data, data access and application development. It draws on Oracle AI Database, Autonomous AI Database and the APEX AI Application Generator. Deep Data Security enforces access policies inside the database, and Deep Trust Data APIs limit how agents can work with data. Oracle also expanded its Autonomous AI Lakehouse capabilities, with updates to data integration and multicloud infrastructure. Juan Loaiza, who leads AI database technologies, said enterprises can't get the benefits of AI unless they can trust what it produces. The conference runs through October 29.

Impact on the tech industry: Oracle is arguing that agent governance belongs in the database, where it holds the system of record, and not in the agent or app layer. That puts it up against Salesforce, ServiceNow and Databricks' governance pitches. The partner opportunity is in integration and security services. SpaceX · xAI · Apollo · Financing · Oct 25–26

SpaceX Closes a $40 Billion Lease Deal to Buy Nvidia Vera Rubin GPUs, Calling It the Largest Committed GPU Financing Ever

Apollo and Goldman Sachs led the master finance lease, with commitments from BlackRock, Goldman Sachs Asset Management and KKR. The GPUs will support xAI, and deliveries start in the first half of 2027.

Read more

Musk said on October 25 that SpaceX had closed about $40 billion in GPU debt. A regulatory filing and reports on October 26 gave the details. It is a master finance lease: Apollo will buy Nvidia "Vera Rubin" GPUs and related equipment in tranches and lease them to SpaceX on terms of roughly 5 to 5.25 years. Apollo and Goldman Sachs led the committed financing, with Goldman as structuring agent and BlackRock, GSAM and KKR affiliates also committing. Heise, citing Reuters, says the deal should close by year-end, and leases begin when equipment arrives in the first half of 2027. The capacity will support xAI and related data-center projects. The deal is bigger than the $36 billion structured-notes deal this summer that bought GPUs for lease to Anthropic, which Broadcom backstopped. Reports of the financing first appeared in early October.

Impact on the tech industry: Private credit is now the main source of funding for frontier compute, but only for borrowers with large non-AI revenue. That gives xAI a scale advantage just as smaller GPU clouds lose access to debt. It also secures Nvidia's 2027 Rubin demand. Watch lease accounting, Apollo's exposure, and whether OpenAI or Meta copy the structure.
