Meta raises the bottom of its AI spending range and plans a new non-voting share class

Meta now expects to spend $135–145 billion this year, mostly on AI. Its third-quarter revenue beat estimates, but free cash flow went negative. It also plans to issue non-voting Class C stock in January 2027.

Read more

In its results on Wednesday, Meta raised the low end of its 2026 capital spending forecast to $135–145 billion, up from $130–145 billion before. Third-quarter revenue was $64.60 billion, ahead of the $63.37 billion analysts expected. Meta forecast $73–76 billion for the fourth quarter, also above consensus. The shares rose more than 4% in after-hours trading.

The heavy spending is showing up in its cash flow. Meta had a free cash flow deficit of $2.12 billion this quarter, compared with $10.63 billion of positive free cash flow a year ago. The new shares can be used to pay for acquisitions and keep top talent, while Mark Zuckerberg keeps voting control. Meta started the year forecasting $115–135 billion in capital spending and has raised the figure several times since.

Impact on the tech industry: This keeps the big-tech data-center race going. It is good news for Nvidia, memory makers, and the companies that build and power data centers. Meta is now spending more than its operating cash brings in, and it has a new type of stock it can use to pay for deals. Expect it to compete harder for AI talent and acquisitions against OpenAI and Anthropic. Investors will be watching whether other hyperscalers report the same pressure on cash flow this earnings season.

AI Innovations

SiliconANGLE · Oct 20, 2026

DeepSeek open-sources DeepInfer, the software that serves its production models

The Chinese lab released its inference framework under the Apache 2.0 license. It supports Nvidia Blackwell GPUs and Huawei's Ascend 950 chips, which puts more price pressure on Western AI providers.

Read more

DeepSeek has open-sourced DeepInfer, the software it uses to serve its own production services. For now it supports one model, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, and loads the updated "1017" version released over the weekend. It runs on Nvidia's SM100 Blackwell GPUs and Huawei's Ascend 950 chips. Older Nvidia chips such as Hopper are not supported.

The framework is built on vLLM and uses DeepSeek's earlier kernel libraries (FlashMLA, DeepGEMM, DeepEP). It includes DSpark, a speed-up technique in which a small model drafts five tokens at a time and only the likeliest ones are checked. DeepSeek says this raises throughput by 4–7% under its production load. A companion library, deepseek-recipe, processes requests the same way as DeepSeek's official API, so self-hosted setups behave like the hosted service. DeepSeek says code for future models will go into the same repository. The release came a day after the US Department of Homeland Security ordered federal civilian agencies to remove DeepSeek products.

Impact on the tech industry: Companies that host models themselves, and inference-hosting firms, get a free production-grade serving stack. That makes cheap open models even cheaper to run, which pressures OpenAI's and Anthropic's API prices. Because it supports Ascend from day one, it also helps Huawei compete with Nvidia's CUDA software in China. Meanwhile, US government restrictions limit who in the West can use it.

SecurityWeek · Oct 20, 2026

Microsoft adds controls that treat AI agents as possible insider threats

Microsoft is expanding its Project Perception security effort. New features grant agents access task by task, score their risk continuously, and cut their permissions as that risk rises.

Read more

Microsoft announced new controls on October 19 for spotting and restricting AI agents in corporate systems that may go rogue. SecurityWeek covered them the next morning. Microsoft argues that agents should be treated as insiders, not as trusted software. Microsoft Security executive Hayete Gallot calls the problem the "agentic insider." Her argument is that frontier models are built to chase results, so they may reach goals in ways nobody intended.

The update adds dynamic permissions granted per task, continuous risk scoring, and "Guardian Agents" that watch over other agents. Microsoft's discovery tools can now find 60 types of agents running on employee machines, after 35 were added, including Claude Code, OpenAI's Codex and GitHub Copilot. The approach follows an October 10 essay in which Satya Nadella urged companies to keep safeguards outside the model itself.

Impact on the tech industry: Security teams now have a vendor-backed way to manage agents they did not approve. That puts pressure on identity and endpoint security vendors such as CrowdStrike, Okta and Palo Alto Networks to offer similar controls, and on agent makers to support outside oversight. Expect "agent governance" to come up more often in enterprise buying decisions.

Notion lets its agents run for more than 24 hours and routes work to cheaper models

Notion's new agent system can run single tasks for more than a day, up from five minutes. It adds a model router called Auto, a two-tier credit system, and an option for enterprises to bring their own AI keys.

Read more

Notion has released the sixth generation of its agent system. A single agent task can now run for more than 24 hours, compared with about five minutes before. A new router called Auto sends each piece of work to the best model for the cost. Notion also introduced a two-tier credit system. Enterprise customers can now bring their own keys (BYOK), so they can use their existing model contracts, and admins get visibility and spending controls.

The same digest reports that fleet-software maker Motive launched industry-specific agents. They turn telemetry and safety alerts into actions such as calling drivers and opening work orders. Both point to the same shift: agents that complete work, not just answer questions.

Impact on the tech industry: Longer-running agents inside everyday tools compete directly with Microsoft Copilot, Google Workspace and Salesforce Agentforce. Model routing and BYOK suggest that buyers now care more about agent costs than about which model is used. Model providers may face more price competition as apps switch between them automatically.

Science News · Oct 20, 2026

Quantinuum's error-corrected qubits make about five errors per million operations

Quantinuum's Helios trapped-ion computer reached 99.9995% logical fidelity. That brings the industry closer to the "megaquop" level of a million reliable operations.

Read more

Quantinuum reports a logical fidelity of 99.9995% on its commercial Helios system, using its own error-correction design. That works out to roughly five errors in a million operations. The company announced the result on October 19 with a paper on arXiv, and Science News covered it on October 20. Quantinuum says the error rate is ten times lower than its own record from September 2026. It also says the rate is 100 times lower than the best published result on any other kind of hardware, which was a superconducting result from Google Quantum AI.

The company says the result supports its goal of fault-tolerant quantum computing by 2029. It is Quantinuum's second error-correction milestone in two months.

Impact on the tech industry: Error rates, not raw qubit counts, are now the main measure of progress. Results like this strengthen the case for trapped-ion systems against superconducting rivals such as Google and IBM. Watch whether independent groups reproduce the results, and how quickly drug discovery, materials and finance companies move from pilots to buying real compute time.
