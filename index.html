EU Leaders Hold Their First Summit on AI in Dublin, With Mistral's Arthur Mensch Making the Case for European AI Sovereignty

At an informal meeting, the heads of government discussed competitiveness, AI sovereignty, labor markets and safety. Estonia and Finland called for EU-level arrangements to respond to serious AI incidents. Officials said Nvidia was not on the agenda but could still come up.

Read more

EU heads of state and government met informally in Dublin on Friday, November 13, for their first discussion focused on artificial intelligence. According to the European Council, Mistral AI CEO Arthur Mensch joined the leaders to discuss Europe's place in the global AI market and the choices needed to strengthen its competitiveness and AI sovereignty. Leaders also discussed AI's effects on Europe's social model and labor markets, and the safety, security and trust problems it raises. Council President António Costa pointed to the AI Act as the world's first regional AI governance framework, which Europe can use to help shape global standards. France 24 reported that Geoffrey Hinton was also scheduled to address the leaders. It also reported that Estonia and Finland asked in a letter for EU-level arrangements to handle serious AI incidents. An EU official told AFP that Nvidia was not formally on the agenda. Foreign Policy notes that Europe is expected to miss its chip-manufacturing targets and that talks about Ukraine risked taking attention away from AI.

Impact on the tech industry: AI policy is moving up to the level of national leaders, which favors European model makers such as Mistral and the push for sovereign compute. While the US is caught up in export-control fights and lawsuits seeking to freeze AI development, Europe is presenting the AI Act as a model for others. Watch whether the leaders' discussion leads to formal European Council conclusions, an EU-wide AI incident-response system, or new chip and compute funding.

TradingView (Seeking Alpha) · AI Weekly · OpenAI · Robotics · Nov 13–14

OpenAI Dissolves Its Standalone Robotics Team for the Second Time and Moves the Work Into Core Research and Applied Groups

Fortune reports that robotics researchers now report to chief research officer Mark Chen, and applied robotics and hardware staff move to President Greg Brockman's Applied group. OpenAI says robotics is still part of its long-term strategy.

Read more

OpenAI confirmed to Fortune that it has broken up its standalone robotics team. Seeking Alpha's summary on TradingView says research staff will now report to chief research officer Mark Chen. Applied robotics and hardware staff move into the Applied group, which reports to President Greg Brockman. OpenAI said it is not giving up on robotics. AI Weekly notes that this is the second time in six years OpenAI has disbanded a robotics team. It wound down its first effort in 2020, rebuilt a team in 2024 and partnered with humanoid maker Figure, a partnership that later ended when Figure started building its own models. According to AI Weekly, OpenAI justified the change with a simulation benchmark in which GPT-6 Astra scored 74.8% on robotics tasks, compared with 51% for specialized models. Researchers quoted by Fortune disagreed about what the move means. One said robotics is "no longer a special problem," while others argued that startups with real-world deployment experience still have an edge.

Impact on the tech industry: OpenAI is betting that general-purpose models, not dedicated robotics stacks, will run robots. If that is right, it puts pressure on humanoid and robot-foundation-model startups such as Figure and on the investors funding them. If it is wrong, those companies benefit. Watch for robotics features in OpenAI's general models, hardware partnerships under Brockman, and how investors value robotics companies such as Agile Robots, which this week reportedly raised money at an $8.5 billion valuation.

Starship Flight 15 Catches the Ship After It Returns From Orbit for the First Time, but the Super Heavy Booster Is Lost in the Gulf

What's new since last edition: SpaceX got half of the planned double catch. Ship 42 reached orbit, deployed 26 Starlink satellites, flew for about 22.5 hours and was caught by the tower arms at Starbase. The booster missed its catch.

Read more

Starship lifted off from Starbase, Texas, on November 13 at 22:47 UTC carrying Starlink Group 31-2, KeepTrack reports. The upper stage reached orbit and, according to Space.com, deployed all 26 Starlink satellites. It then made the longest Starship test flight so far. On November 14, Ship 42 returned to Starbase and was caught by the launch tower's arms, which NewSpace Economy says is the first tower catch of a Starship returning from orbit. The flight lasted about 22.5 hours. The Super Heavy booster did not meet the requirements for a catch. It was diverted offshore, its flight termination system activated, and it was lost in the Gulf of Mexico. NewSpace Economy says the available evidence does not show that the guidance system caused the failure. Space.com noted earlier that it was unclear whether an FAA ground stop would affect the ship's reentry plans.

Impact on the tech industry: Catching the ship after an orbital flight is a key step toward a fully reusable heavy launcher. A reusable Starship would sharply cut the cost of deploying Starlink and of launching the orbital compute that SpaceX is planning alongside its $74 billion ground data-center program. Competing launch providers and satellite-broadband rivals such as Amazon Kuiper face a faster-moving SpaceX. Watch the booster investigation, the inspection of Ship 42, and the timing of Flight 16.

OpenAI Launches ChatGPT Wallet So Its "Dots" Agents Can Buy Things for Users With Tokenized Visa, Mastercard and Amex Cards

The feature is available first to US ChatGPT Pro subscribers. Agents never see the actual card number, and users approve each purchase. OpenAI says it is not taking a cut of transactions for now.

Read more

OpenAI announced ChatGPT Wallet on Friday, November 13. It lets the company's "dots" agents, which launched at its developer conference in late September, complete purchases for users, CNBC reports. Android Authority says the feature is limited to US-based Pro subscribers with a US-issued Visa, Mastercard or American Express card. Users add a card once, let a dot help with a purchase, and then approve it. The wallet charges the card with a payment token instead of the card number, security code and expiry date, and it keeps a record of each purchase. AI Weekly, citing CNBC, says the agent gets a separate token for each transaction. OpenAI also explained how merchants can avoid accidentally blocking its agents. Commentary on Nov 14 said OpenAI is publishing a Commerce Partner Playbook to help merchants recognize legitimate agent traffic, and that it plans to let users set spending limits so they don't have to approve every purchase. OpenAI says it is not taking a share of transactions to start.

Impact on the tech industry: Together with this week's Businesses in ChatGPT launch, OpenAI now covers the whole path from finding a business to paying it. That puts it in competition with Google, Amazon and payment companies over who owns AI-driven checkout. The card networks gain a role, while retailers that block bots, such as United Airlines, may come under pressure to change course. Watch fraud and dispute rates, a wider rollout beyond Pro, and when OpenAI starts charging fees.
