Marvell Agrees to Buy Lumentum in a $115 Billion All-Stock Deal, Then Both Stocks Fall Sharply

This is one of the largest tech takeovers on record. It combines Marvell's networking and custom chips with Lumentum's lasers and optical parts for AI data centers. Investors reacted badly: Marvell fell almost 13% and Lumentum 18% on Monday.

Read more

Marvell said on Monday, November 2, that it will acquire optical-components maker Lumentum. The deal is all stock and values Lumentum at an enterprise value of about $115 billion, or about $102.5 billion in equity, according to Reuters. Lumentum holders get 4.5 Marvell shares per share, a premium of about 23.9% to the October 28 close. Marvell shareholders will own about 67% of the combined company. The companies expect more than $1 billion in annual cost savings and say the deal will add to Marvell's adjusted earnings per share in the first year. Closing is expected in the first half of 2028. Marvell would owe Lumentum $5.8 billion if it walks away, or $3.2 billion if regulators block the deal. The deal is more than ten times the size of Marvell's previous largest purchase, the $10 billion Inphi deal in 2021. Nvidia invested $2 billion in each company earlier this year.

Impact on the tech industry: This is the biggest bet yet that optical networking will limit how far AI clusters can scale. It sets Marvell up to compete with Broadcom as a single supplier of data-center hardware. It also brings two key Nvidia suppliers under one roof. The sell-off shows how little patience investors now have for AI spending. Watch antitrust reviews, including China's, and how Coherent and other optical suppliers respond.

Drax and Brookfield Plan a Gigawatt-Scale AI Data Center at a North Yorkshire Power Station, With Investment That Could Pass £20 Billion

The project is being built with Radiant, Brookfield's AI infrastructure platform, and backed by a Brookfield-Qai joint venture. Phase one would receive £2.5 billion and serve customers from early 2028. Drax shares rose more than 8%.

Read more

On Tuesday, November 3, Drax Group said it had signed an agreement with Brookfield and Radiant to develop, deliver and operate data-center capacity in phases at the Drax Power Station site. The project will be backed by a joint venture between Brookfield and Qai. Drax says the first phase is up to 100MW and could receive £2.5 billion. CNBC reports that phase one will use grid power and serve commercial customers from early 2028. The long-term target is more than 1GW. Brookfield estimates total investment, including chips, could exceed £20 billion (about $26.8 billion). The partners welcomed the UK government's new Data Centre Covenant, which aims to speed up power connections and planning. Just Security, citing BBC research, notes concern that the UK's data-center push could depend on more natural gas and work against its net-zero goal.

Impact on the tech industry: Putting data centers on existing power-station sites is becoming Europe's fastest route around grid-connection delays. That favors operators that already have land and generation capacity. It also adds a large UK neocloud competitor to Nscale and the hyperscalers. Watch for the anchor tenant, the power mix, and planning approvals.

Security Roundup: Northflank Tells Customers to Rotate All Secrets After AI API Keys Are Misused, and a 9.8-Severity Linux Kernel Flaw Is Not Yet Fixed on Ubuntu

Update on Northflank: the breach was detected by Wiz, and stolen keys were used to make unauthorized AI API calls. Check Point also flags an actively exploited Brotli decompression flaw and a Japan Airlines breach through Amadeus.

Read more

CISO Series reports that Northflank disclosed its breach on Saturday after cloud-security firm Wiz detected it. Attackers used customers' AI API keys without authorization. Northflank told customers to assume their environment variables had been decrypted and to rotate every credential stored on the platform. Check Point's November 2 weekly report lists several other issues. They include a critical Linux kernel flaw, CVE-2026-98432, rated 9.8 on the CVSS severity scale, and HTTP2Shell (CVE-2026-111497), a remote-code-execution flaw in Brotli decompression that attackers are already using. Japan Airlines disclosed a third-party breach affecting about 92,000 passengers through the Amadeus reservation system. Florida's education department disclosed a breach that may affect 4.9 million people. Deep Gains says Red Hat has shipped kernel fixes, but Ubuntu is still listed as vulnerable. Separately, the UK's cyber chief warned of AI-driven attacks on critical infrastructure by 2028.

Impact on the tech industry: Platform-as-a-service providers now hold AI API keys that are worth stealing, so a breach turns directly into compute bills and risk to downstream systems. Linux fleets and Node.js services need urgent patching. Watch Northflank's root-cause report and Ubuntu's patch timing.
