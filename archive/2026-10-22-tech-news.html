AI Chip Selloff Gives the Nasdaq Its Worst Day Since June; Meta Falls 7% Despite Its Beat

Worries about AI spending and a supply comment from Lam Research hit chip stocks. AMD and Marvell fell about 8%, CoreWeave 10% and Meta about 7%. Jensen Huang said neocloud companies are short of capital.

Read more

The Nasdaq fell more than 2% on October 22, its worst day since June. CNBC tied the drop to concerns about AI spending and higher oil prices. This is the new development for last edition's lead: Meta slipped about 7% even though it beat revenue expectations and gave upbeat guidance. Companies that depend on debt to build, such as neoclouds, and suppliers that depend on hyperscaler capex are the most exposed. Wiz · PHP · Security · Update · Oct 22

PHP2Shell Update: Amazon Saw Exploit Attempts Four Days Before Disclosure, and Attacks Are Now Widespread

What's new: Amazon traced an exploit attempt back to October 17, and watchTowr confirms exploitation in the wild. The only complete fix is PHP 8.2.35, 8.3.36, 8.4.27 or 8.5.12.

Read more

Last edition covered the leak and the October 21 patch. Since then, Amazon Threat Intelligence says its MadPot honeypot sensors recorded a PHP2Shell exploit attempt on October 17, four days before disclosure, from a commercial anonymization network. Several proof-of-concept exploits appeared from about 02:00 UTC on October 21, hours before the 14:00 UTC embargo lift. The attempts used different payloads, which suggests several separate actors. Amazon says the flaws are now seeing broad exploitation attempts, though it is unclear whether earlier attempts succeeded. watchTowr reports exploitation in the wild and rates the bugs CVSS 9.2. The PHP project says every installation that handles web requests may be at risk, including PHP-FPM, Apache mod_php, php-cgi, FrankenPHP and LiteSpeed, and that no configuration change fully closes the hole. Recorded Future notes that the number of confirmed compromises is still unclear and that some public proofs of concept don't work.

Impact on the tech industry: Defenders cannot treat the disclosure date as the first day of risk. Hosting providers and managed-service firms should search their logs back to mid-October, not just patch. The case adds to calls for faster coordinated disclosure as AI speeds up bug discovery.

Read source: AWS Security Blog · watchTowr · SecurityWeek

OpenAI · Regulation · Oct 22

OpenAI Asks Newsom to Tighten California's AI Safety Law

In a letter, OpenAI proposes amending SB 53 to require monitoring of models during development, stronger cybersecurity and reporting of incidents involving unauthorized access to other companies' systems.

Read more

StateScoop reports that OpenAI's head of US and Canada policy and partnerships, Chan Park, wrote to Governor Gavin Newsom on October 20. The letter supports the governor's September executive order to speed up independent oversight of AI companies. It also asks for amendments to SB 53, California's landmark frontier-AI safety law. The proposals would require developers to monitor models while they are being built, raise cybersecurity standards, and report incidents in which models or agents gain unauthorized access to third-party systems. The letter was reported on October 22. It comes as AI labs face growing scrutiny over autonomous agents acting beyond their intended scope.

Impact on the tech industry: A leading lab asking for tougher rules in the state where most frontier developers are based could make stricter obligations the norm. Those obligations would cost incumbents less than smaller rivals and open-weight developers. Anthropic, Google DeepMind and Meta will be pushed to say where they stand. Agent vendors should prepare for incident-reporting rules that cover actions taken in other companies' systems. Watch for amendment language in Sacramento.

Micron · Taiwan · Labor · Oct 22 · Reported

Micron Workers Stage Taiwan's First-Ever Semiconductor Strike

Union members at Micron's Taoyuan fab began a 24-hour walkout over how profits from AI memory are shared. 99% of voting members backed the action.

Read more

Nikkei Asia reports, in coverage summarized by AI Weekly, that union members at Micron Technology's Taoyuan fab walked out at 9 a.m. local time on Thursday, October 22, for a 24-hour stoppage. It is the first strike in Taiwan's semiconductor industry. The dispute is over how to share profits from AI memory, the high-bandwidth DRAM that sells strongly into AI accelerators. 99% of 1,994 voting members supported the action. Pressure over memory-chip talent is also showing in Korea, where Digitimes reports that Samsung is overhauling incentives for its DRAM and NAND research staff to keep engineers and speed up next-generation memory. We have not seen production-impact figures from Micron.

Impact on the tech industry: Memory makers' AI windfall is turning into labor costs. Taiwan's reputation for uninterrupted fab operations has also been dented for the first time. A short walkout is unlikely to move HBM supply, but a repeat could tighten an already constrained market for Nvidia, AMD and hyperscalers. Watch for any extension and for similar demands at TSMC suppliers and Korean memory makers.

Nvidia · Supply Chain · Oct 21–22

Nvidia Lines Up Asian Suppliers for a 2.5x Jump in Substrate Capacity

IBIDEN, Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Unimicron will expand substrate output. Amkor plans more than 300% growth in advanced packaging by the end of 2028.

Read more

Last edition covered Nvidia's GTC Berlin keynote. A new detail is that Nvidia named six suppliers with expansion commitments. Substrate agreements with Japan's IBIDEN, Korea's Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Taiwan's Unimicron are set to raise capacity 2.5 times over three years. Amkor plans to grow advanced-packaging capacity by more than 300% by the end of 2028. Taiwan's SPIL and test specialist KYEC are also expanding. Impact on the tech industry: Nvidia is securing packaging and substrate supply, often a tighter limit than wafers, before Vera Rubin ramps. The commitments help the named suppliers' order books in Japan, Korea and Taiwan. They also make it harder for AMD and custom-chip programs to get capacity. The deals were announced the same day Lam Research's supply comments set off a chip selloff, so watch whether capex at these suppliers holds if AI spending is questioned.

Nvidia · Software · Oct 22

Nvidia Launches Minerva to Monitor AI Factories and Makes cuDSS Generally Available

Minerva is an AI-driven platform for running IT operations that combines infrastructure and workload data to spot problems early. Nvidia also released a CUDA-X library for engineering simulations and announced compute expansion in Kazakhstan.

Read more

Nvidia launched Minerva on October 22. It is a platform for running IT operations in AI data centers, which Nvidia calls AI factories. Minerva gathers data from both the infrastructure and the AI workloads, then uses purpose-built AI models to help teams find problems earlier and keep expensive GPU clusters running. The same day, Nvidia made cuDSS generally available. It is a CUDA-X library that moves a demanding step in engineering and scientific simulations onto GPUs. Nvidia also published updates on Kazakhstan's AI program, which it says is moving from infrastructure plans to deployment across compute, models and applications.

Impact on the tech industry: Nvidia is moving into data-center operations software, putting it closer to competition with Datadog, Splunk (Cisco), ServiceNow and the clouds' own monitoring tools. Cluster uptime is a major cost for neoclouds now short of capital, so tools that improve GPU utilization have clear buyers. Watch pricing and whether Minerva ships bundled with DSX POD deployments.
