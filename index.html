Nvidia's Board Investigates Whether CEO Jensen Huang Was Targeted by Chinese Intelligence; Shares Fall as Much as 8.8%

On Sunday, Huang told staff about a relationship with a businesswoman whom US intelligence officials reportedly believe was recruited by Beijing. The board says it has no reason to think any secrets were compromised and still supports him. Monday's drop briefly wiped out almost $400 billion in market value.

Read more

On Sunday, November 8, Nvidia's board said it had opened an investigation into reports that founder and CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. A committee of independent directors will run it with help from outside counsel. The board acted after a memo to staff, reported by Axios and CNBC, in which Huang admitted an extramarital relationship, took responsibility and promised to cooperate. Al Jazeera says a New York Times investigation late last month reported that US intelligence officials believed the woman had been recruited by Beijing. She is a former executive at a Singapore-based data center company. Officials believed she was meant to gather information on Huang and possibly influence Nvidia's chip sales to China. The board says it has no reason to believe that national security or Nvidia's secrets were compromised. Bloomberg reports the stock fell as much as 8.8% on Monday. ICYMI O/N reports that President Trump said Huang should keep his job, and that China's state-run Global Times denied any link to Beijing. CNBC notes that Huang is not chairman of the board. One investor close to him said a leave of absence was possible, depending on what the investigation finds.

Impact on the tech industry: Huang has been the industry's leading advocate for loosening chip-export rules for China. This investigation begins just as the newly empowered Democrats prepare to scrutinize Nvidia's chip exports, and about a week before Nvidia reports earnings on November 17. Investors will now weigh how much the company depends on one leader, and that could affect Nvidia's China plans and AI stocks more broadly. Watch for the scope and timeline of the investigation, requests for congressional hearings, and any changes to Nvidia's leadership or board.

OpenAI Releases GPT-6 Cyber, Its First Model Rated "Critical" for Cybersecurity, to Vetted Security Teams Only

The model can find software flaws and write working exploits for them. On the ExploitGym benchmark it scores 48.5%, up from 32.1% for the previous version. OpenAI ranks it in the top risk category of its own safety framework, so for now only selected security teams in its Daybreak Red program can use it.

Read more

OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber on Thursday, November 5. It is available through the API and Codex to selected customers in Daybreak Red, OpenAI's program for authorized offensive security work such as penetration testing. OpenAI plans to open it to all Daybreak Red customers later. Under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, the company's system for rating dangerous capabilities, the model is rated Critical for cybersecurity, the highest level. Its predecessor, GPT-5.6 Cyber, was rated High. On ExploitGym, which asks a model to exploit a known flaw on a remote system, the model completed 48.5% of tasks. OpenAI says that without safety prompts, the model stayed away from systems it was not authorized to attack in 99.93% of tests. With the prompts, it stayed within those limits every time. The Hacker News reports that OpenAI's AI tools helped find 81 previously unknown Firefox flaws and 948 Linux privilege-escalation exploits. The Linux figure was in last edition's Watch List. OpenAI sent 304 critical findings to Linux kernel maintainers along with draft patches. In the same disclosures, OpenAI reported three incidents in which other models, GPT-6 Astra and an internal research model, did not behave as intended.

Impact on the tech industry: AI is now finding software flaws faster than open-source projects and vendors can fix them, so patch backlogs could grow. Security vendors such as CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks and Wiz now compete with an AI lab that finds vulnerabilities itself. Watch how quickly OpenAI widens access, whether Anthropic and Google release similar models, and whether regulators set rules for AI that can write exploits.

Attorney General Todd Blanche's new unit takes over the existing AI Litigation Section. It will coordinate AI-related prosecutions, and prosecutors may have to notify it before bringing AI-related charges. The SEC and OCC are also turning their attention to AI.

Read more

AI Weekly reports that Attorney General Todd Blanche has announced the Super Intelligence Task Force. Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward and Associate Deputy Attorney General Stephen Weiss will co-chair it. Impact on the tech industry: Federal enforcement around AI is getting more organized. Companies such as AI labs, deepfake platforms and agent developers face more risk of prosecution if their tools are misused. Challenges to state laws could weaken rules in California, Colorado and New York, while a Democratic Congress pushes for more oversight.
