Vendor Releases · Meta · Oct 21

Meta Raises Its AI Spending Floor and Creates Non-Voting Class C Stock to Pay for It

Q3 revenue rose 26% to $64.6 billion, but capital spending exceeded operating cash flow. Meta now plans to spend $135-145 billion on capex this year and has set up a new share class it can use to raise money.

Read more

Meta reported third-quarter revenue of $64.60 billion, up 26% and above its own $61-64 billion guidance. Capital expenditures, including finance-lease payments, were $34.91 billion. Operating cash flow was $32.79 billion, so free cash flow was negative $2.12 billion. Meta raised the bottom of its 2026 capex range, which moves from $130-145 billion to $135-145 billion. It also raised its expense outlook to $175-179 billion, which includes a $9.9 billion charge tied to an agreement with US state attorneys general. Headcount fell 15% year over year to 66,775.

Each Class A and Class B share will receive one non-voting Class C share, which works like a two-for-one split. The new shares take effect January 19, 2027, and trading starts in late January. Meta says the class gives it flexibility for raising capital, paying employees and making acquisitions. CFO Susan Li said there are no specific financing plans yet. Fortune reports that the structure followed board talks about a possible California wealth tax and Mark Zuckerberg's voting control.

Impact on the tech industry: Even the richest platforms are now spending more on AI than their operations bring in, and they are building ways to fund the gap with equity. That supports demand for Nvidia, memory makers and data-center suppliers into 2027. It also puts pressure on other hyperscalers to explain how they will pay for their buildouts. Watch whether Meta uses Class C stock to buy companies.

Anthropic · Agents · Oct 21

Anthropic Launches Claude Duet, an Agent That Acts Before You Ask

Duet runs on Opus 5.5. It watches connected apps, sends a morning brief and handles tasks in its own cloud browser, and it asks for approval before high-stakes actions.

Read more

Claude Duet is a standalone product in research preview with a waitlist. It is open to Pro and Max subscribers in the US on the web and through a dedicated iOS app. Each morning it sends a short brief from connected apps, then checks those apps every few hours and alerts the user when something needs attention. Tasks run in a cloud virtual machine with its own browser, so no local computer is required. Jobs range from admin work to building web apps. Duet asks for user approval before sending messages, making payments, signing documents or deleting files. It does not see passwords, and it uses a dedicated classifier to guard against leaks of sensitive data. Background checks count toward the user's plan limits.

Impact on the tech industry: With Meta's Muse agent and OpenAI's Dots, all three leading labs now sell proactive personal agents to professionals and small businesses. Productivity-suite vendors and agent startups face direct competition. Enterprise buyers will judge these agents on permission controls and approval gates, the same governance issues raised by last edition's Microsoft Guardian Agents story.

Wiz · PHP · Security · Oct 20–21

"PHP2Shell": AI-Assisted Research Finds Critical Flaws in PHP Itself

Two critical bugs in PHP's core engine allow remote code execution before login on WordPress, Drupal and Joomla. Details leaked a day before the October 21 patch.

Read more

Wiz Research says it found CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 with help from OpenAI's frontier models. Both are memory-safety bugs in PHP's core engine, so they do not depend on the application running on top. According to Wiz, every PHP release back to version 5.4 has at least one of the flaws. Wiz demonstrated remote code execution without authentication on WordPress, Drupal and Joomla. It also showed a separate memory-disclosure path that could expose passwords, tokens, API keys and certificates. Huntress reports the details leaked online on October 20, before coordinated disclosure and before a fix existed. The PHP project released patches on October 21, and some websites went offline for emergency updates.

Impact on the tech industry: Wiz says PHP powers about 70% of websites, so hosting providers, CMS vendors and managed-service firms now face a broad patching job. The case is also a clear example of AI models finding deep, long-hidden bugs in core infrastructure. Expect defenders and attackers to use AI more for finding vulnerabilities, and patch windows to get shorter.

Nvidia Pitches Container-Built AI Factories, More Autonomous Cars and a Reference Humanoid to Europe

DSX POD aims to shorten Vera Rubin deployments from six months to 14 days. Volvo adopts Hyperion, and Agile Robots' Agile ONE SN becomes a reference humanoid for Nvidia's Isaac GR00T robotics platform.

Read more

In his keynote at GTC Berlin, Jensen Huang said the Vera Rubin platform is in full production. He announced DSX POD, a reference design for complete Vera Rubin AI factories that partners build inside modular containers and test before delivery. Nvidia says on-site deployment can drop from six months to 14 days once power and other utilities are ready. In cars, Volvo is adopting the Hyperion platform and Alpamayo software for automated driving. Huang also said upcoming vehicles from Volkswagen (vans), JLR, Volvo and Mercedes-Benz will be Hyperion-compatible. The Agile ONE SN, a 1.4-meter, 40-kilogram humanoid from Agile Robots with 31 degrees of freedom and a Jetson Thor computer on board, becomes a reference robot for Isaac GR00T.

Impact on the tech industry: Nvidia is selling Europe a full package of compute, vehicles and robots. If prefabricated AI factories work as promised, the main bottleneck shifts from construction to power and permits. European carmakers are increasingly building on Nvidia, so their driving software depends more on a US supplier. That adds to the sovereignty debate around last edition's Germany–Nvidia declaration.

Third-quarter revenue reached $14.58 billion and SpaceX shares jumped. Musk also said the jointly run Terafab AI chip project expects a test chip by mid-2027.

Year-Old AI Lab Flapping Airplanes Raises $1 Billion, Reportedly at $6 Billion

Index and Lux co-led the Series A, nine months after a $180 million seed round. The lab is betting on models that need far less training data.

Read more

Flapping Airplanes announced a $1 billion Series A co-led by Index and Lux. Reports put the valuation at $6 billion; the company has not confirmed that figure. Its $180 million seed round from GV, Sequoia and Index, nine months ago, valued it at $1.5 billion, so the reported valuation has quadrupled. The lab wants to build intelligent systems without ingesting huge portions of the internet. It argues that much more data-efficient models would be cheaper to train and run, and easier to deploy across the economy. The new money will go mainly to hiring.

Impact on the tech industry: Investors are paying for alternatives to the brute-force scaling that drives today's compute race. If data-efficient training works, it could lower the cost of entry for new model builders. It could also reduce dependence on licensed data and weaken the advantage of the largest labs. For now this is a research bet without a shipping product.
