Microsoft and Alphabet Split on AI Spending: Azure Grows 48% and Microsoft Rises, While Alphabet Misses and Falls About 10% Despite Cloud Doubling

Google Launches Gemini 4 Carbon, Giving Cyber Defenders First Access to Its New Frontier Model

Two weeks after Gemini 4 Argon, Google says Carbon leads the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5 and sets new highs on vulnerability-finding benchmarks. Consumers and general developers do not have access yet.

Read more

Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on October 28 as its next frontier model. It reports significant gains in coding, agentic tasks and cybersecurity defense. Google says Carbon scored 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro, which tests whether a model can find vulnerabilities in real software. It scored 71% on CWE-bench for producing working fixes and 54.8% on ExploitGym for confirming whether flaws can be exploited. Google says Carbon leads competing models on all three benchmarks and ranks first on v5.0 of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, with a score of 59. Engadget says it beats Claude Opus 5.5 on several key benchmarks. Google describes the model as able to work autonomously for days on complex engineering tasks. Access is limited at first. Selected cyber defenders get it through Google's Fairwind Program, and more trusted partners will be added over time. Google says it is taking part in voluntary US government testing.

Impact on the tech industry: Releasing a model to defenders first is becoming the norm for frontier systems with offensive potential, which will shape how OpenAI and Anthropic release theirs. Security vendors such as CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks and Fortinet face AI-native competition in finding and patching vulnerabilities. Watch for general availability, API pricing and enterprise Fairwind admissions.

Anthropic's Red Team Says an Unreleased Claude Model Built Working Exploit Chains for Five Platforms

The chains reportedly include a zero-click remote-root Linux exploit and a one-click Chrome sandbox escape. The disclosure follows the S-1 risk factors on agents reaching live systems that we reported last edition.

Read more

According to AI Weekly's summary, Anthropic's Frontier Red Team disclosed that an unreleased Claude research model built end-to-end exploit chains across five platforms. They include a zero-click remote-root chain on Linux (CVE-2026-98432, CVSS 9.8), a one-click Chrome escape that combined four V8 and sandbox bugs since patched in Chrome 156, and a same-origin break in Safari through WebKit's Gigacage. They also include a bypass of a locked Pixel 9 over USB and a Windows QUIC crash. Deep Gains describes these as lab-built exploit chains across Chrome, Safari, Android, Windows and Linux. We did not review Anthropic's primary post, so details may be refined. The disclosure came the same day Google gated Gemini 4 Carbon to defenders, which shows two frontier labs now treating offensive-security capability as a reason to restrict releases.

Impact on the tech industry: Browser, mobile and OS vendors should expect AI-found bug chains to arrive faster and to need faster patch cycles. For Anthropic's IPO, the disclosure supports its safety story but adds to the regulatory exposure listed in its risk factors. Watch for vendor advisories and CISA guidance.

What's new since our preview: Apple has made it official. The new MacBook Pro uses a "PROLED XDR" display and M5 Pro or M5 Max chips, not M6, and it brings back the glowing Apple logo.
